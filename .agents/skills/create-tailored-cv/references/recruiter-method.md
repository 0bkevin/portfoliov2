## 1. Goal and review model

The CV has one job: earn an interview by showing that the candidate meets the minimum qualifications for a job title. It is not a biography, a graphic-design exercise, or a list of technical brags.

Assume the first recruiter pass lasts about 15 to 20 seconds. The first half of page one must make the target title and the strongest qualifications obvious.

Tailor a small set of CVs to job titles such as `Full-Stack Software Engineer`, `Backend Engineer`, `AI Engineer`, or `Data Engineer`. Do not rewrite the candidate's identity for every employer. A posting is useful for ranking qualifications, but the reusable title is the organizing principle.

## 2. Selecting qualifications

Read the bullet points under headings such as:

- Requirements
- Qualifications
- Must Have or Need to Have
- What We Are Looking For
- You Might Be a Great Fit If

Ignore benefits, company promotion, day-to-day filler, and EEO text when building the qualification list.

For rigorous title research, collect 10 to 15 good postings with the same title, count repeated qualifications, and rank them by frequency. The published [Qualification sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/e/2PACX-1vRg7oze-BnheKtSvQH2ApktuRYyWaXOuvE9hgke4puccxX4Gs5I9-xAfxaKgRoYxYh6W1DlyqSA9e2c/pubhtml#gid=1421544187) indexes job titles and review episodes. Search it by title and use the linked material as supporting research, not as a substitute for the current posting.

Prioritize qualifications the candidate can prove. A keyword in a skills section is not evidence.

## 3. Turning qualifications into evidence

A usable qualification contains:

1. The qualification or technology.
2. What the candidate built or changed with it.
3. How and where it was used.
4. The non-technical reason, user need, or business outcome.

Example:

> Built a multi-tenant talent platform with TypeScript, Node.js, PostgreSQL, React, and Next.js so advisors could manage candidates, training services, and program reporting in one system.

This demonstrates more than the isolated words `TypeScript`, `PostgreSQL`, and `React`.

Use three to six relevant terms in a bullet when they fit naturally. Fewer may waste space; more often becomes a technical inventory that hides the work.

Aim to cover 75% of the important qualifications. Never fill a gap with adjacent experience. A candidate who used Supabase Auth has not necessarily designed Supabase Row Level Security. A Go project does not prove Rust experience.

## 4. Content rules

- Use reverse chronological order for normal work histories.
- Give each substantive role a plain-language overview followed by evidence bullets.
- Use three to eight bullets depending on tenure and relevance. Short roles usually need fewer.
- Put professional experience before projects. Keep a project to one to three bullets unless it is the candidate's primary evidence for the title.
- Keep the basic qualifications visible. Do not bury TypeScript, JavaScript, HTML, CSS, React, REST APIs, SQL, cloud, Git, or CI/CD behind fashionable details when the title requires them.
- Explain collaboration with stakeholders and communication to non-technical people through a real situation.
- Use dates with months and years for employment and internships. Projects do not require dates.
- Avoid unsupported numbers. Acceptable numbers usually describe sales, leadership scope, or infrastructure/user scale with meaningful context.
- Avoid arbitrary percentages, vague claims such as “improved efficiency,” and numbers without a baseline.
- Do not add a standalone skills section merely to collect keywords. Place qualifications in evidence bullets.
- Add a short summary only to explain relocation, work authorization or visa conditions, or an industry transition.
- State work authorization only when the candidate has confirmed it.
- Do not change official job titles or dates to match the posting.
- Keep education concise. Put it at the bottom when it is not required or may distract from relevant experience.

### The hamburger test

The requested qualification must appear directly. Adjacent experience is not a substitute. If a role asks for Node.js, a bullet about a Python API is relevant backend experience but does not prove Node.js. Label the gap honestly and use direct Node.js evidence elsewhere.

### The portability test

Remove any sentence that could be copied unchanged into another person's CV. Replace it with the actual product, users, data, failure, decision, or operating context.

## 5. Formatting rules

- Arial only.
- Name at 14 pt, body at 10.5 pt.
- Single column and 1.5 line spacing.
- US Letter unless the user requests another paper size.
- Black and white; blue is allowed for phone, email, LinkedIn, GitHub, and portfolio links.
- No photo, icons, graphics, sidebars, progress bars, or multi-column layouts.
- Section names should be bold; job information should be italicized; dates should align right.
- Two pages are acceptable for an experienced engineer. Readability is more important than forcing one page.
- Balance the pages. Large blank areas created by a premature page break are a layout defect.

## 6. Application heuristics

Application order matters more than elapsed posting time. A role posted days ago may still have few applicants; a remote engineering role can receive more than 100 applications quickly. Prefer roles with fewer than roughly 100 applicants or clearly reposted openings.

Use interview yield as feedback on the CV and targeting strategy. Remote and international searches generally produce lower response rates, so evaluate a meaningful sample rather than a handful of applications.
